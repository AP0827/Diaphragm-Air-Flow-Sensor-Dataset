07 — Key Engineering Insights

Production and Architectural Learnings

Critical takeaways regarding the sensor integration and analytic pipeline design.

Insight 01
Feature engineering yields primary predictive value

Deriving temporal differentials (Delta) provides strong predictive signal, whereas expanding raw feature dimensionality (Polynomial regression) leads to overfitting without improving generalization.

Insight 02
Dynamic changes drive flow physics

Correlation analysis shows that flow is strongly linked to sensor delta changes (+0.615) rather than raw absolute levels (-0.237), confirming that pressure differentials are physically dominant.

Insight 03
Automated preprocessing safeguards pipeline integrity

Filtering repeated headers, malformed rows, and out-of-bounds readings ensures input sanitization.

Insight 04
Independent validation reveals operational shifts

Out-of-session testing prevents overconfidence by exposing baseline drifts (~140k in Raw, 3x increase in vibration noise) typical of real-world deployments.

Insight 05
Workflow is fully reproducible

The pipeline script automates the entire sequence from raw ingestion to model benchmarking and diagnostic report compilation, ensuring consistency and auditability.

09 — Limitations & Future Work

Operational Limitations and Calibration Strategy

Identified pipeline limitations and proposed mitigations for next-generation revisions.

  • Coordinate Baseline Drift: Absolute sensor offsets change significantly between sessions. Future models should employ relative scaling or rely strictly on dynamic differences.
  • Rig Vibration Coupling: AccY/Z noise variances increased 3-4x in the validation session. Adaptive filtering or vibration-based noise cancellation should be introduced.
  • Dynamic Calibration: Under shift, absolute linear coefficients degrade. A dynamic self-calibration or online zero-point adjustment step is required to maintain accuracy in long-term runs.
